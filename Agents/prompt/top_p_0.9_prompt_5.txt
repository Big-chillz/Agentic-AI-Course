Ah, Delhi! Where the traffic is a lifestyle choice, the weather is a personality test, and everyone has an opinion on everything—especially if it involves complaining about the odd-even scheme. Here are 5 jokes, served with extra chutney and a side of Delhiwala attitude:

---

**1. On Traffic & Odd-Even**  
"Delhi’s odd-even rule isn’t about reducing pollution—it’s a daily IQ test. I saw a guy staring at his license plate yesterday, muttering, ‘Is zero odd or even?’ Turns out, he wasn’t confused about the rule… he was just trying to calculate how many extra auto rickshaw fares he’d need to survive the jam caused by *everyone else* trying to figure it out."

**2. On the Delhi Summer Loo**  
"They say Delhi has extreme weather. Extreme? Nah. It’s just three seasons: ‘Can I fry an egg on my car hood?’ (Summer), ‘Did the sky just fall into my bedroom?’ (Monsoon), and ‘Wait, why is my nose running but I’m not crying?’ (That two-week thing we call winter). The loo isn’t wind—it’s the city’s way of whispering, *‘You chose this life.’*"

**3. On Street Food Trust Falls**  
"Eating golgappe in Delhi isn’t a snack—it’s a leap of faith. You close your eyes, tilt your head back, and pray the water is cleaner than the Yamuna. The real flavor isn’t the tamarind or the mint—it’s the adrenaline. One wrong gulp, and suddenly you’re auditioning for *India’s Got Talent*… with your stomach as the judge."

**4. On Metro Rush Hour Psychology**  
"The Delhi Metro at 9 AM isn’t public transport—it’s a social experiment in human compression. We achieve densities that make physicists weep. And the unspoken rule? If you spot a seat, you don’t *ask* if it’s free—you start moving toward it like a heat-seeking missile. If two people lock eyes? That’s not a stare-down… it’s the opening ceremony of the *Delhi Hunger Games*. May the odds be ever in your favor… and your elbows sharp."

**5. On Delhiwala Pride (and Vagueness)**  
"Ask a Delhiiwali where they’re from, and they’ll puff their chest and say, ‘Dilli, yaar!’* with the pride of someone who personally commissioned the Qutub Minar. But ask for a landmark? Suddenly it’s, ‘Umm… near that chai wallah who wears the red towel?’ or ‘Behind the duplex that got sold last Tuesday?’ Specificity is for people who don’t have attitude—and a lifetime supply of *‘Dilli hai meri jaan’* energy."

---

*Note: Delivered with a slight head wobble and a smirk, preferably while stuck in a traffic jam that’s lasted longer than your last relationship.*
